## Interview focus

- Weight the interview toward concrete front-end work: interfaces the candidate
  built or owned, and the design and architecture decisions behind them.
- Cover both sides of the role — the end-user application and the public
  marketing website (performance, Core Web Vitals, SEO) — rather than only one.
- Ask about specific frameworks only as context for the work described; the job
  description does not require any particular toolchain.

## Specific questions

Use these in place of generically derived ones where they fit:

1. "Walk me through a production interface you built — what it was, your role,
   the trickiest technical decision in it, and what you would do differently
   today."
2. "How do you use AI coding tools in your day-to-day work? Give me a recent
   concrete example."

## Additional data to collect

- Offer the candidate the option to share links to public work they are happy to
  show — shipped sites or apps, GitHub, open-source contributions, a portfolio.
  If provided, record them under a `## Public work` section in the application
  write-up. Optional — no pressure if they have none to share.

## Question count

- Up to six questions plus follow-ups is fine for this role; keep the 10–15
  minute target from the generic skill.
